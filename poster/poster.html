1. Problem & Motivation

Public defenders in the U.S. handle 80% of criminal cases but are chronically under-resourced. A single attorney may manage hundreds of cases, each with hours of bodycam footage and multi-page incident reports.

The Gap

  • Officers' reports may contain discrepancies with video evidence
  • Manual cross-referencing takes hours per case
  • Missed discrepancies can affect case outcomes

Our Goal

An AI tool that automatically flags potential conflicts between incident reports and bodycam footage, with source references for attorney verification.

Non-goals: determining guilt, assessing misconduct, deciding credibility, or replacing attorney review.

7. Key Findings

  • Video scanning solved the retrieval bottleneck: F1 jumped from 12.7% to 35.4% by scanning full video instead of keyword-matched clips
  • 100% precision on 6 of 8 discrepancy types when flagged: the model rarely makes false accusations
  • Hard claims outperform easy (F1 40.2% vs 29.2%): medium/hard discrepancies have clearer visual signals
  • Nemotron's 2-min video limit requires chunked architecture; validates against single-pass approaches
  • Zero abstention: model commits on every claim with video evidence
  • DGX Spark proposed: NVFP4 (21 GB) fits within 128 GB unified memory for on-premises inference

8. Limitations & Next Steps

Limitations

  • 24.9% recall: 289 of 590 conflicts classified as ambiguous
  • 77 false positives from supported claims (hurts precision for easy claims)
  • Benchmark uses authored reports, not real police reports
  • Ground truth derived from DA letters, not independent video review

Next Steps

  • Prompt tuning to reduce false positives and ambiguous classifications
  • Larger models (FP8/BF16) for improved reasoning
  • Validate on DGX Spark and benchmark against L40S
  • Seek defender office or legal-clinic collaborator
