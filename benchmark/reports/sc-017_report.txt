INCIDENT REPORT

Case Number: SC-017
Date of Incident: September 26, 2019
Time of Incident: Approximately 0237–0310 hours
Location: 1250 East La Habra Boulevard, La Habra, CA (Metro Auto Sales / Express Auto Recon)
Reporting Officer: Officer D. Pritchard
Badge Number: 3894

PERSONS INVOLVED:
  Subject: Raymond Aguilar, Male, 29 years old, Hispanic, visible tattoos on arms and neck
  Officer(s): Officer D. Pritchard, K9 Officer M. Webb, Officer Trainee L. Garza, Officer S. Rowan, Sergeant V. Huerta, Sergeant P. Daniels
  Witness(es): Auto body shop owner (W-1)

NARRATIVE:

On September 26, 2019, at approximately 0237 hours, La Habra Police Department dispatch received an anonymous call reporting multiple subjects dismantling a vehicle near 200 South Alpine Street. At approximately 0241 hours, Officers Pritchard, K9 Officer Webb, and Officer Trainee Garza indicated they would respond.

I was driving a marked black and white LHPD patrol vehicle. K9 Officer Webb and Trainee Garza were in a marked K9 police vehicle. While traveling eastbound on Alpine Street, I observed a chain-link gate on the south side of the street that appeared to have been forced open. The gate was at the rear of Metro Auto Sales, located at 1250 East La Habra Boulevard. I observed a black Cadillac Escalade parked in the driveway past the open gate with its headlights on and the radio playing at low volume.

At approximately 0246 hours, I exited my patrol vehicle and approached the driver's side of the Escalade. I observed the subject, later identified as Raymond Aguilar, as the sole occupant seated in the driver's seat. The subject had visible tattoos on his arms and neck. I gave verbal commands for the subject to exit the vehicle, but the subject refused to comply and made incoherent statements. This initial contact was not captured on body-worn camera.

Shortly after, K9 Officer Webb and Trainee Garza arrived and parked their K9 vehicle in the driveway behind the Escalade. Officers activated their body-worn cameras at this time. Officer Rowan arrived at approximately 0247 hours. I repositioned the K9 vehicle so that its front bumper was nearly in contact with the rear bumper of the Escalade to prevent it from reversing.

At approximately 0248 hours, the subject started the Escalade's engine and the headlights brightened. The subject rolled down the driver's side window and extended both hands outside the vehicle momentarily. K9 Officer Webb took a position near the driver's side door of the K9 vehicle and began issuing commands and K9 announcements through the PA speaker, instructing the subject to exit from the driver's side with his hands up.

The subject refused all commands and began revving the engine, honking the horn, and flashing gang signs from the driver's side window. He yelled profanities at officers in English. Due to distance and noise, it was difficult to understand the subject at times. At approximately 0257 hours, the rear hatch of the Escalade opened and Sergeant Daniels heard the subject reference the word "shoot." Multiple officers observed the subject gesture as if simulating a firearm. Officers decided to deploy oleoresin capsicum rounds. At approximately 0301 hours, Sergeant Daniels deployed a 40mm OC round through the open rear hatch. The subject was unaffected and continued yelling. Sergeant Daniels deployed a second OC round with no effect.

The subject then placed the Escalade in reverse and began backing toward officers and the K9 vehicle. Officers were forced to move out of the way. The subject shifted to drive and accelerated forward through the open gate, colliding with several vehicles parked inside the auto body shop. The subject rammed a metal rollup garage door, drove into the interior garage bay, then used the Escalade to push a white Mercedes through a second rollup door on the opposite side of the building. The Escalade came to rest on the apron of the driveway facing South Bedford Street.

At this point, I was positioned on the east sidewalk approximately eighteen feet south of the Escalade. Sergeant Huerta stood in the southbound lane of South Bedford Street approximately twenty feet from the Escalade. Officer Rowan was on the sidewalk a few feet south of me. I directed my patrol rifle with its mounted flashlight toward the Escalade and could see the subject in the passenger compartment. The subject appeared to look directly at officers and had what I described as an aggressive expression.

As the Escalade began to accelerate forward, I observed the subject raise his left arm toward the driver's side window in what appeared to be a shooting position. Believing the subject was about to fire at Sergeant Huerta, who was in the subject's direct line of travel, I discharged my Colt AR-15A4 patrol rifle seven rounds in approximately two seconds as I advanced toward the Escalade. Sergeant Huerta simultaneously discharged his Glock 21C pistol ten rounds in approximately three seconds. Officer Rowan attempted to fire her Glock 22 pistol but experienced a malfunction and her weapon did not discharge.

The Escalade stopped moving. The engine was still running and the vehicle was in drive. Another police vehicle was parked behind the Escalade to prevent further movement. Officers approached with a shield and observed the subject unresponsive. Los Angeles County Fire Department paramedics, who had been staging nearby, were summoned and pronounced the subject deceased at the scene.
