INCIDENT REPORT

Case Number: SC-009
Date of Incident: March 11, 2021
Time of Incident: Approximately 1818 hours to 1839 hours
Location: Pacific Coast Highway, Laguna Beach, CA
Reporting Officer: Officer D. Kessler
Badge Number: 3891

PERSONS INVOLVED:
  Subject: Tyler Brandt, Male, approximately 30 years old, White, black sweatshirt, light blue t-shirt underneath
  Officer(s): Officer D. Kessler, Corporal A. Novak (PIT maneuver), Officer M. Trujillo, Sergeant R. Holt
  Witness(es): None at scene of shooting

NARRATIVE:

On March 11, 2021, at approximately 1818 hours, I was driving southbound on Pacific Coast Highway in my marked Laguna Beach Police Department patrol vehicle when I observed a white Mercedes sedan traveling southbound at a high rate of speed. I attempted to pace the Mercedes and observed erratic driving behavior, including veering on and off the shoulder. Based on these observations, I believed the driver was potentially impaired.

I broadcast over the radio my intent to conduct a traffic stop and requested immediate backup at approximately 1819 hours. The driver of the Mercedes initially appeared to comply, pulling the vehicle onto the northbound shoulder of Pacific Coast Highway. I exited my patrol vehicle and began approaching the vehicle on the passenger side. As I reached the rear quarter panel of the Mercedes, the driver abruptly accelerated away from the traffic stop, making a hard-right turn across oncoming traffic.

A vehicle pursuit ensued. During the pursuit, the subject collided with two civilian vehicles, causing one vehicle to overturn. I continued the pursuit with emergency lights and sirens activated. Additional Laguna Beach Police units joined the pursuit, and an Orange County Sheriff helicopter provided aerial support. The subject reached speeds of approximately 90 miles per hour and was observed driving on the wrong side of the road and swerving into oncoming traffic.

Laguna Beach Police officers successfully deployed a spike strip, which caused all four tires of the Mercedes to deflate. Despite the tire damage, the subject continued to flee. The subject continued driving on deflated tires for approximately two additional miles before Corporal Novak successfully executed a pursuit intervention technique (PIT) maneuver, bringing the Mercedes to a stop in the southbound lane of Pacific Coast Highway. The vehicle came to rest facing north.

My patrol unit, Officer Trujillo's unit, and Sergeant Holt's unit took positions approximately 15 feet from the stopped Mercedes. All units had their takedown lights and spotlights illuminated and directed at the subject. I was positioned in the center, with Officer Trujillo to my left and Sergeant Holt to my right.

Upon the vehicle coming to a stop, I observed the subject remain seated in the driver's seat for approximately 30 seconds, appearing to look around the interior of the vehicle. The subject then exited the vehicle and immediately turned to face the officers. The subject placed his left hand behind his back and made a distinct reaching motion toward his left hip area. I interpreted this movement as consistent with someone reaching for a concealed firearm.

I commanded the subject to get on the ground. The subject did not comply and instead began advancing toward me and the other officers in a rapid, deliberate manner. As the subject closed the distance, he suddenly extended both arms forward at chest height with his hands together in a manner that simulated pointing a firearm directly at me. Believing I was about to be shot, I discharged my department-issued Glock 17 firearm, firing four rounds at the subject at approximately 1839 hours.

Despite being struck, the subject remained on his feet and continued advancing toward the officers. The subject yelled profanities at the officers while continuing to make threatening gestures consistent with pointing a weapon. At this point, Officer Trujillo deployed his department-issued Taser, which struck the subject and caused him to fall to the ground. Sergeant Holt broadcast over the radio that shots had been fired and a Taser had been deployed.

I approached the subject with Officer Trujillo, and we placed the subject in handcuffs. We immediately began rendering first aid. I conducted a search of the subject's person and recovered a small black semi-automatic pistol from the subject's right front waistband area. Laguna Beach Fire Department paramedics arrived at approximately 1848 hours and transported the subject to Mission Hospital in Mission Viejo, where he underwent surgery. The subject survived his injuries, which included two gunshot wounds to the upper torso and one to the left forearm.
