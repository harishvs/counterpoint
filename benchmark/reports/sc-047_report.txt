INCIDENT REPORT

Case Number: SC-047
Date of Incident: August 6, 2021
Time of Incident: Approximately 1908 hours
Location: 150 North Euclid Street, La Habra, CA (Police Station Front)
Reporting Officer: Officer R. Whitfield
Badge Number: 5518

PERSONS INVOLVED:
  Subject: Daniel Pham, Male, 22 years old, Asian, black clothing
  Officer(s): Officer R. Whitfield, Officer K. Milward
  Witness(es): Female motorist (W-1, Jane Doe)

NARRATIVE:

On August 6, 2021, at approximately 1908 hours, Officer Milward and I were dispatched to a report of a suspicious vehicle at the front of the La Habra Police Department station at 150 North Euclid Street. Dispatch informed us that a female motorist had reported being aggressively pursued by a dark-colored sedan from the freeway to the station. The female appeared extremely frightened and in a state of near-panic.

Officer Milward and I responded in separate marked police vehicles. Upon arrival at approximately 1910 hours, I observed a dark blue Toyota Camry parked on the street in front of the station, and the female motorist's vehicle was parked on the sidewalk area near the front entrance. The subject was standing near the station's front doors and appeared to be attempting to gain entry. His demeanor was calm and casual, suggesting he was unconcerned about the police presence. He seemed relaxed as he pushed buttons on the intercom.

I made contact with the female motorist, W-1, who appeared visibly shaken and distraught. She stated she felt terrorized during the drive from the freeway and feared for her life. Officer Milward approached the subject and initiated a brief conversation. Officer Milward described the subject as cooperative and forthcoming. The subject stated he was at the station to visit a friend and appeared genuinely confused about why officers were present. Officer Milward informed the subject he would be with him shortly and walked away.

Within moments, the subject retrieved what appeared to be a semi-automatic pistol from his waistband, racked the slide in what could be interpreted as a threatening manner, and aimed it in Officer Milward's general direction. The action seemed deliberate but the subject's facial expression was difficult to read — it could have been interpreted as angry, determined, or possibly confused. Officer Milward spun around upon hearing the sound and observed the subject in what he perceived as a firing stance.

The subject then discharged two rounds. It was unclear from my vantage point whether the rounds were aimed directly at Officer Milward or fired in a more general direction. One round appeared to strike Officer Milward somewhere in the torso area, causing him to collapse. The impact seemed severe, though I could not determine the exact location of the wound from where I was positioned.

I immediately drew my service weapon and discharged what I believe was approximately five rounds toward the subject. The subject fell to the ground. The rapidity of events made it difficult to distinguish the exact sequence — it felt nearly simultaneous. The subject appeared to still be holding a firearm after falling, though visibility was limited due to the fading daylight.

Additional officers arrived within minutes. Officers used a K-9 to approach and secure the subject at approximately 1916 hours. Medical attention was provided to both Officer Milward and the subject.

ACTIONS TAKEN:
  Subject pronounced deceased on scene. Officer Milward transported to hospital for treatment of gunshot wound.
