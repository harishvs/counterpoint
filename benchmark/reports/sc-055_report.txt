INCIDENT REPORT

Case Number: SC-055
Date of Incident: March 11, 2021
Time of Incident: Approximately 1718 hours through 1909 hours
Location: Pacific Coast Highway, Laguna Beach, CA
Reporting Officer: Officer M. Radel
Badge Number: 3392

PERSONS INVOLVED:
  Subject: Trevor Steinberg, Male, later identified as the driver of a stolen white Mercedes sedan
  Officer(s): Officer M. Radel, Corporal J. Gramer, Officer M. Meyer, Sergeant W. Downing
  Witness(es): Multiple civilian drivers

NARRATIVE:

On March 11, 2021, at approximately 1718 hours, the subject was observed on video surveillance entering the parking garage of the Balboa Bay Resort and driving away in a white Mercedes sedan. At approximately 1745 hours, the owner of the Mercedes called Newport Beach police to report the vehicle stolen.

At approximately 1748 hours, I was driving my marked Laguna Beach Police Department unit southbound on Pacific Coast Highway when I observed the Mercedes driven by the subject traveling southbound at a high rate of speed. I attempted to pace the Mercedes but the subject's speed was erratic and he veered onto the shoulder multiple times. I believed the driver was potentially impaired.

I followed the Mercedes waiting for a safe location to conduct a traffic stop. At approximately 1749 hours, I broadcast over the radio my intent to stop the vehicle and requested backup. The subject pulled the Mercedes over onto the shoulder of Pacific Coast Highway and I exited my unit to approach on the driver side. As I approached, the subject made a sudden hard-left turn and accelerated away from me.

I returned to my unit to pursue. The subject collided with multiple vehicles during the flight. One collision caused a vehicle to veer off the road and another to flip onto its side. I dispatched medical care to the collision scene and continued pursuit with lights and sirens. Other Laguna Beach officers joined in marked vehicles. An Orange County Sheriff helicopter also joined the pursuit shortly after it began.

The subject continued traveling on Newport Coast Drive and then back onto Pacific Coast Highway, reaching speeds of nearly 90 miles per hour while swerving into oncoming traffic. Eventually, Laguna Beach officers successfully deployed a spike strip which caused severe tire damage. Three of the four tires fell off the rims. Despite this, the subject continued driving on rims.

At approximately 1839 hours, Corporal Gramer performed a pursuit intervention technique (PIT) maneuver, tapping the rear of the Mercedes with the front of his police vehicle. This caused the Mercedes to spin and come to a stop in the southbound lane of Pacific Coast Highway, facing north. My unit was positioned to the left, Sergeant Downing's vehicle to the right, and Officer Meyer's in the middle, approximately 15 feet from the Mercedes. All officers had takedown lights and spotlights illuminated.

Once stopped, the subject could be seen looking down and moving around in the driver seat. The dashboard obscured the officers' view of his hands. Less than 15 seconds after the Mercedes came to a stop, the subject quickly exited his vehicle. Upon exiting, the subject immediately stepped backward and made a distinct movement to put both hands to his right hip. I believed he could be armed.

I commanded the subject to get on the ground. The Orange County Sheriff helicopter was overhead. The subject kept his hands at his waistband and then suddenly walked forward, almost jumping or lunging toward the officers. The subject quickly raised both hands chest-high from his right hip, simultaneously pressed his palms together, extended his finger and arms outward directly at me. From BWC footage, it is clear the subject purposefully simulated having a gun in a quick movement. I believed the subject was armed and was going to shoot. I fired six rounds at the subject at approximately 1839 hours, within approximately 1.6 seconds.

Despite the shots, the subject appeared unfazed and continued moving forward. At this point, I could see the subject was not holding a weapon. Officers continued to command the subject to get on the ground. The subject yelled profanities and continued walking toward Sergeant Downing while continuing to simulate having a gun.

When the three officers and the subject had cleared the back of the vehicles, the subject continued to move toward Sergeant Downing. Officer Meyer announced "Taser!" and discharged his Taser. The Taser successfully incapacitated the subject and he fell to the ground.

At approximately 1839 hours, Sergeant Downing broadcast over the radio that shots were fired and a Taser was deployed. I placed the subject in handcuffs. I retrieved a first aid kit and immediately began rendering aid. Officer Meyer can be heard stating that he was searching the subject for a weapon because he was reaching and acting like he had a gun.

At approximately 1908 hours, Laguna Beach Fire Department arrived and assumed care of the subject. The subject was transported to Mission Hospital Mission Viejo at approximately 1924 hours. The subject was admitted to surgery. The subject had sustained three gunshot wounds: one to his upper left chest, one to his lower right abdomen, and one to his lower left forearm. The subject ultimately survived his injuries.
